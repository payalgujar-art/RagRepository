HR POLICIES AND EMPLOYEE GUIDELINES

Document: HR_Policies.txt


1. DATA SECURITY

Data security refers to protecting company and employee information from unauthorized access, modification, disclosure, or destruction.

Employees must protect confidential company information and must not share sensitive information with unauthorized individuals.

Employees should use strong passwords and must not share their passwords with colleagues.

Company data should only be stored on approved company systems and applications.

Employees must immediately report suspected data breaches, lost company devices, unauthorized access, or accidental disclosure of confidential information to the IT or Information Security team.

Employees should not copy confidential company information to personal devices, personal email accounts, or unauthorized cloud storage.

Examples of confidential information include employee personal information, salary information, customer information, business strategies, source code, credentials, financial information, and internal company documents.


2. POSH POLICY

POSH stands for Prevention of Sexual Harassment.

The company is committed to providing a safe and respectful workplace free from sexual harassment.

Sexual harassment includes unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

Examples may include unwelcome physical contact, inappropriate comments, sexually suggestive messages, unwanted requests for sexual favors, inappropriate jokes, or sharing sexually explicit material.

Employees who experience or witness sexual harassment are encouraged to report the incident through the company's designated reporting channels.

All complaints should be handled with confidentiality and sensitivity.

Retaliation against an employee who makes a complaint in good faith or participates in an investigation is not permitted.

Employees are expected to maintain professional and respectful behavior toward colleagues at all times.


3. WORKING HOURS

The standard working schedule is Monday through Friday.

Employees are expected to follow the working hours defined by their department or project.

Employees should communicate with their manager when they expect to be unavailable during scheduled working hours.

Project-specific working schedules may differ depending on business requirements, customer requirements, or team arrangements.

Employees working remotely are expected to remain available through approved communication tools during agreed working hours.


4. LEAVE POLICY

Employees may request leave for vacation, personal requirements, illness, or other approved reasons.

Leave requests should be submitted through the company's approved leave management system.

Employees should obtain manager approval before taking planned leave.

For unexpected absence due to illness or an emergency, employees should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible.

The number of available leave days depends on the employee's employment terms and applicable company policy.

Employees should plan extended leave in advance to minimize disruption to project activities.


5. WORK FROM HOME POLICY

Employees may work remotely when permitted by their department or manager.

Employees working from home must maintain a suitable working environment and protect company information.

Company laptops and other company equipment must not be used by unauthorized individuals.

Employees must connect to company systems using approved security mechanisms.

Confidential conversations should not be conducted where unauthorized people can hear them.

Employees must report lost or stolen company equipment immediately.


6. CODE OF CONDUCT

Employees are expected to behave professionally and respectfully.

Discrimination, harassment, bullying, intimidation, and retaliation are not acceptable workplace behaviors.

Employees must comply with applicable company policies and procedures.

Employees should avoid conflicts of interest and disclose potential conflicts to their manager or the appropriate HR representative.

Company resources should be used responsibly and primarily for legitimate business purposes.

Employees are responsible for protecting company property, information, systems, and intellectual property.


7. EMPLOYEE PERFORMANCE

Employee performance is evaluated based on responsibilities, objectives, quality of work, collaboration, communication, and professional behavior.

Managers should provide employees with regular feedback regarding their performance.

Employees who receive performance feedback are encouraged to discuss expectations, development opportunities, and areas for improvement with their manager.

Performance improvement plans may be used when an employee's performance does not meet established expectations.

A performance improvement plan may define specific objectives, timelines, support, and evaluation criteria.


8. RECRUITMENT AND ONBOARDING

New employees must complete the required onboarding activities.

Employees may be required to provide employment-related documents during the onboarding process.

New employees should complete mandatory training assigned by HR, Information Security, or their department.

Employees are expected to familiarize themselves with relevant company policies and procedures.

Managers are responsible for helping new employees understand their role, responsibilities, team structure, and project expectations.


9. INFORMATION SECURITY TRAINING

Employees may be required to complete periodic information security training.

Security training may cover password security, phishing awareness, data protection, device security, access control, and incident reporting.

Employees should not click suspicious links or open unexpected attachments from unknown sources.

Suspicious emails or security incidents should be reported using the company's approved security reporting process.

Employees must complete mandatory security training within the assigned deadline.


10. CONFIDENTIALITY

Employees may have access to confidential information while performing their job responsibilities.

Confidential information must only be accessed when required for legitimate business purposes.

Employees must not disclose confidential information to unauthorized persons.

Confidential information remains protected even after an employee leaves the organization.

Employees must return or securely delete company information and assets when required by company policy or during separation.


11. EMPLOYEE GRIEVANCES

Employees who have workplace concerns may raise them through their manager, HR representative, or the company's designated grievance process.

Employees are encouraged to raise concerns promptly so that they can be reviewed appropriately.

HR may investigate workplace complaints and may request information from relevant employees.

Employees participating in an investigation are expected to provide accurate information and maintain confidentiality.


12. TRAVEL AND EXPENSES

Business travel must be approved according to the company's travel approval process.

Employees should use approved travel providers when required.

Business expenses must be supported by appropriate receipts or documentation.

Expense claims should be submitted through the approved expense management system within the required timeframe.

Personal expenses are not reimbursable unless specifically permitted by company policy.


13. IT EQUIPMENT

Company-issued laptops, phones, and other equipment are provided for authorized business purposes.

Employees are responsible for taking reasonable care of company equipment.

Employees must not install unauthorized software on company devices when prohibited by IT policy.

Employees should report hardware problems, lost devices, or suspected security compromises to the IT team.

Company devices must be returned when employment ends or when requested by the company.


14. EMPLOYEE SEPARATION

Employees leaving the organization must complete the required separation and exit procedures.

Employees may be required to return company equipment, identification cards, documents, and other company property.

Access to company systems may be disabled as part of the separation process.

Employees must continue to protect confidential company information after leaving the organization.

Final settlement and other employment-related documentation will be processed according to applicable company procedures.


15. IMPORTANT CONTACTS

For HR-related questions:
Contact the HR department or assigned HR representative.

For data security incidents:
Contact the Information Security or IT team immediately.

For workplace harassment concerns:
Use the company's designated POSH reporting mechanism or contact the appropriate Internal Committee.

For technical issues:
Contact the IT help desk through the approved support channel.
